      How Receipts checks a document

      1. Find the claims. Nemotron 3 Nano reads the document and pulls out every sentence that relies on a specific source, resolving numbered and author-year references against the bibliography.
      2. Find and read the source. Links are read with Tavily Extract, reranked to the claim so only the relevant passages come back. Cases, papers and books are located with Tavily Search, and Nano decides which result is the cited source itself.
      3. Judge. Nemotron 3 Super compares the claim with the source passages and must copy the exact sentences its verdict rests on.
      4. Verify the evidence. Receipts checks that every quoted sentence really appears in the source. Quotes the model can't ground lower its confidence.
      5. Second opinion. Anything flagged, low-confidence or ungrounded goes to Nemotron 3 Ultra, which can overturn the first verdict. Ultra also writes the summary.

      What the verdicts mean

      Supported
      The source says what the document says.
      Partly supported
      The document overstates it or drops a qualifier.
      Misrepresented
      Right source, wrong details: a number, holding or quote.
      Contradicted
      The source says the opposite.
      Not found
      No copy of the source exists online. Possibly invented.
      Can't verify
      The source couldn't be read (paywall, offline, print-only).

      Limits. Receipts gives evidence, not a ruling. Print-only books and paywalled articles often come back as "Can't verify", and a "Not found" can also mean a badly formatted citation. Always open the source before acting on a flag.
